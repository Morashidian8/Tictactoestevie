# Food close-up: macro, steam, a slow pour

A filled 7-part prompt for a food shot that looks edible: one macro framing, one slow physical event (a pour, steam, a crack), side light that shows texture, and the hands and labels to keep out.

Category: generation · difficulty: beginner · time: 10 min · works with: kling, veo, runway, image-gen · verified: False
Source: https://superduperai.co/library/food-macro-close-up-video-prompt/

## Prompt / recipe

```
SUBJECT: {the dish or ingredient, with texture words: glossy, crumbly, charred, flaky, and one colour}
ACTION: {one slow physical event, e.g. 'honey pours in a thin thread onto the stack' / 'steam rises and curls' / 'a knife cracks the caramel crust'}
SETTING: a dark wooden table, a linen napkin half out of focus, a ceramic plate, a kitchen in the background fully blurred
CAMERA: macro close-up, 30 degrees above the plate, static, the subject filling two thirds of the frame; the only motion is the event itself
LENS & LIGHT: 100mm macro look, very shallow depth of field with the focus on the nearest edge of the food; one soft window light from the side and slightly behind so steam and gloss catch it, a dark fill on the camera side
STYLE: editorial food photography in motion, true-to-life colour with warm highlights, visible texture, light film grain, no HDR look, no oversaturation; the pour or the steam moves at natural speed or slightly slower
SOUND: close foley of the event (a thin pour, a crackle), kitchen room tone; no music
DURATION: 5 seconds, 9:16. No on-screen text, no hands, no cutlery moving, no packaging with labels.
```

## Tips

- What it breaks: hands and utensils (fingers around a fork), labels and packaging text, and pour physics when the pour is fast. Keep hands out, keep the event slow, keep the ingredient loose or on a plate.
- Speed: slightly slower than real is the sweet spot; true slow motion makes steam look like smoke and a pour look like syrup.
- Aspect: 9:16 for feeds, with the food in the middle third so the caption and the hook text can sit above it. 1:1 for a listing.
- Try first: Kling or Veo from an image-model still of the plated dish; the still fixes the plating and the model only adds the pour. A suggestion to start with.
